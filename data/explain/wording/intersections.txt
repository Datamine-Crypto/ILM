Note: Points where shapes in a plane meet: line segments, circles and polygons (a polygon is the segments between its corners in order, and from the last corner back to the first). Two segments cross when the ends of each lie on the two sides of the other; the side of a point to a segment is the sign of a cross product. A segment meets a circle at one point when one end is inside and one outside, and at two points when both ends are outside, the point of the segment nearest the centre lies between the ends, and that point is inside: the signs of the quadratic of the segment's points, with no square root. Two circles meet at two points when the distance of their centres lies between the difference and the sum of their radii. A point where shapes touch, a tangent, and a corner that two shapes share count once. The count of points sums each meeting from both shapes, so it is twice the number of points.
